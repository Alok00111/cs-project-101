Why We Chose XGBoost

🏆

Highest Accuracy & F1 Score

XGBoost achieved 59.53% accuracy and an F1 of 55.1% — outperforming all three other models on both metrics. On a 3-class imbalanced dataset this improvement is clinically meaningful.

📈

Best ROC AUC (0.6906)

ROC AUC measures how well the model distinguishes between readmission classes. XGBoost's AUC of 0.6906 vs Logistic Regression's 0.6519 = a 3.9-point improvement in discriminative power — critical in clinical decisions.

⚡

Fast Inference (0.053s)

Despite being an ensemble of 200 trees, XGBoost predicts in just 53ms on 20k records. This makes it suitable for real-time clinical decision support without perceptible delay.

🔍

Handles Class Imbalance Better

XGBoost's boosting mechanism focuses iteratively on misclassified samples. This gives better recall for high-risk patients than Logistic Regression or a single Decision Tree.

🧠

SHAP Explainability

XGBoost is natively supported by SHAP's TreeExplainer, giving us exact (not approximate) Shapley values. This provides per-patient explanations — essential for clinical AI transparency and regulatory compliance.

📚

Literature Benchmark

Published studies on this UCI Diabetes dataset report XGBoost consistently outperforms other classifiers in the 58–65% accuracy range. Our results align perfectly with the literature (Strack et al., 2014 and subsequent papers).

Limitations & Future Work

⚠ Current Limitations

  • Class imbalance: Readmitted class is smaller than Not Readmitted
  • No hyperparameter tuning (GridSearch/Bayesian optimization)
  • Features based on hospital encounter data only, no longitudinal data

✓ Future Improvements

  • SMOTE oversampling for minority class balancing
  • Bayesian hyperparameter optimisation with Optuna
  • Deep Learning (TabNet, MLP) for potential AUC improvement
  • Cross-hospital validation for generalisability
